# JUMBO POP

### A 7-Person English Play

**Adapted from the ice-cream scene in *Zootopia***

**Running time:** about 10–11 minutes
**Level:** senior high school English (short sentences, everyday spoken English)

---

## CHARACTERS

| # | Character | Lines | Notes |
|---|-----------|-------|-------|
| 1 | **Judy** — a rabbit police officer | most | Quick, clear, confident. Sharp and straight to the point. |
| 2 | **Nick** — a fox, street-smart and lazy | second | Slow voice, easy smile. Never in a hurry. |
| 3 | **Elephant Shopkeeper** | about 10 | Loud, rude, short answers. Big voice, small words. |
| 4 | **Little Fox** | about 10, all short | Acts "cute son". Mostly face and body: hopping, licking, grabbing hands. |
| 5 | **Customer A** | about 5 | Shocked reactions. A bit of a gossip. **Doubles as Mouse Worker 1.** |
| 6 | **Customer B** | about 6 | Grumpy, funny. Complains all the time. **Doubles as Mouse Worker 2.** |
| 7 | **Narrator** | about 10, all short | Simple English. Calm and warm. Sets the scene and closes the story. |

**Doubling:** Customers A and B come back in Scene 3 wearing hard hats as Mouse Workers 1 and 2. Their scene-2 lines are finished by then, so only a hard hat and a tiny tool are needed — no costume change. **Mouse Worker 3** is one extra person (backstage crew is fine) with four short lines. If you truly have only seven people, cut him: give "You don't have a paycheck" to Mouse Worker 1 and all of Mouse Worker 3's buying lines to Mouse Worker 2.

---

## SETTING

Three playing areas on one open stage — no set change needed:

- **Stage right:** the street (a bench or a step is enough).
- **Upstage centre:** the ice-cream shop counter. A big menu board above it.
- **Stage left:** the sunny step / construction site (a low block or a box).

Lights up at the start. Narrator stands at the front corner of the stage, half inside the story.

---

## PROPS

Ticket pad and pen · handcuffs (on Judy's belt) · sunglasses (Nick) · one giant fake popsicle ("Jumbo Pop", about 80 cm long) · a small bag holding: a blunt table knife, a shallow pan, a tray of small popsicle molds, wooden sticks · a small money cup · a wallet with a few bills · apron (Elephant) · shopping bags (Customer A) · three hard hats and three tiny tools (Mouse Workers).

**Safety:** use a blunt or wooden knife, and make the "melting" water clearly visible — a small jug of coloured water poured into the pan works better than real ice.

---

# SCENE 1 — THE STREET

**(about 2 minutes)**

*[Lights up. NARRATOR steps forward.]*

**NARRATOR:** Good morning. Welcome to Zootopia — a city of animals. Big animals. Small animals. And one very small rabbit with a very big job.

*[JUDY enters stage right, ticket pad in hand. Fast, cheerful, non-stop.]*

**NARRATOR:** Her name is Judy Hopps. She is a police officer. Today her job is simple: write tickets for cars that park in the wrong place.

**JUDY:** *(writing)* Wrong side of the street. Forty dollars. Have a nice day. *(walks to next spot)* No parking zone. Thirty dollars. Have a nice day.

*[A scooter sound offstage. Judy jumps back.]*

**JUDY:** Hey! Watch it! ...And have a nice day.

*[CUSTOMER A enters stage right with shopping bags, sees Judy.]*

**CUSTOMER A:** Officer, is this your first week? You look… happy.

**JUDY:** *(proud)* Second week. I wrote two hundred tickets yesterday.

**CUSTOMER A:** *(uneasy)* Two hundred. Wow. Good for you. *(walks away fast)*

*[CUSTOMER B enters behind A, sees Judy, stops.]*

**CUSTOMER B:** Two hundred? I got a ticket yesterday. Was that you?

**JUDY:** *(checks pad)* Blue car, no mirror?

**CUSTOMER B:** *(sighs)* That's me.

**JUDY:** *(cheerful)* Then yes! Have a nice day!

**CUSTOMER B:** *(to A, quiet)* She's terrifying.

*[A and B exit into the shop. NICK enters stage right — slow walk, sunglasses, hands in pockets. LITTLE FOX runs in and grabs his hand.]*

**NARRATOR:** Then Judy sees a fox. Sunglasses. Slow walk. He looks like he has never been in a hurry in his whole life.

**NICK:** *(passing Judy)* Officer. Nice day.

**JUDY:** *(narrowing her eyes)* Move along.

**NICK:** I am moving. Very slowly. It's the only speed I know.

**LITTLE FOX:** *(pulling at him)* Dad! Dad! Ice cream!

**NICK:** *(looking down)* Oh, hey, buddy. You found me. Amazing. *(to Judy)* Kids, right?

**JUDY:** *(flat)* He called you Dad.

**NICK:** Because I'm his dad. That's how it works.

**LITTLE FOX:** Dad! Ice cream! Now!

**NICK:** *(sighs)* Okay, okay. *(to Judy, a small bow)* Duty calls.

*[NICK and LITTLE FOX walk toward the shop, upstage centre. JUDY watches them go.]*

**JUDY:** *(to herself)* A fox. A kid. And a whole free afternoon. *(beat)* No. Not free. I'm working. I am working.

*[She follows them, writing in her pad.]*

**NARRATOR:** She is not working. She is curious. There is a difference.

*[Lights change — shop area brightens.]*

---

# SCENE 2 — THE ICE-CREAM SHOP

**(about 3 minutes)**

*[ELEPHANT behind the counter in a small apron, huge and unimpressed. CUSTOMER A and B are in line.]*

**NARRATOR:** The best ice-cream shop on Main Street. Run by an elephant who has stood behind that counter for thirty years. He has heard every order. He has liked none of them.

**ELEPHANT:** *(loud)* Next!

**CUSTOMER A:** One small vanilla, please.

**ELEPHANT:** *(hands it over)* Three dollars. Next!

**CUSTOMER B:** Two scoops of chocolate. *(to A)* I waited twenty minutes for this.

**ELEPHANT:** Four fifty. Next!

*[NICK and LITTLE FOX enter. JUDY slips in behind them and stays by the door.]*

**LITTLE FOX:** *(pointing up at the menu board)* Dad! The Jumbo Pop!

**NICK:** *(looks up)* Huh. That's not a popsicle. That's a building.

**LITTLE FOX:** *(jumping)* The Jumbo Pop! The Jumbo Pop!

**NICK:** *(to Elephant, polite)* Good afternoon. One Jumbo Pop, please.

*[ELEPHANT stops. Slowly looks down over the counter.]*

**ELEPHANT:** ...You.

**NICK:** Me. *(smiles)*

**ELEPHANT:** *(flat)* We don't serve foxes here.

**CUSTOMER A:** *(shocked)* Oh my.

**CUSTOMER B:** *(to A, quiet)* Here we go again.

**NICK:** *(still polite)* Sorry. What was that?

**ELEPHANT:** I said we don't serve foxes. Foxes steal. Foxes lie. Every one of you.

**NICK:** *(calm)* I have money. *(shows a twenty)* Real money. See? The elephant kind.

**ELEPHANT:** *(snorts)* Put it away. I don't want it.

**LITTLE FOX:** *(small voice)* Dad? Why is he angry?

**NICK:** *(crouches down)* He's not angry, buddy. He's just big. Big animals are loud.

**LITTLE FOX:** *(whispers)* I'm hungry.

**NICK:** *(stands up, quiet)* Okay.

*[NICK puts a hand on the Little Fox's head. He speaks to the Elephant, calm but firm.]*

**NICK:** Sir. It's a hot day. My son is hungry. We have money. Please sell us a popsicle.

**ELEPHANT:** *(turns away)* No.

**CUSTOMER A:** *(to Elephant, nervous)* Um. That's… not very nice.

**ELEPHANT:** *(over his shoulder)* Mind your business.

**CUSTOMER B:** *(to A, low)* Don't. He once threw a cone at a giraffe.

*[JUDY steps forward.]*

**JUDY:** Excuse me.

**ELEPHANT:** *(looks down at her)* …What.

**JUDY:** Officer Hopps, ZPD. Is there a problem here?

**ELEPHANT:** *(points at Nick)* He is the problem.

**JUDY:** He asked for a popsicle. He has money. He said please. I heard all three.

**ELEPHANT:** *(scoffs)* He's a fox.

**JUDY:** He's a fox. And this is a shop. Shops are open for business, aren't they? Or does your sign say "No animals allowed"?

**ELEPHANT:** *(uncomfortable)* …No.

**JUDY:** Then I think you should serve your customer.

**CUSTOMER A:** *(quiet, to B)* She's got a point.

**CUSTOMER B:** *(to A)* She's still terrifying.

**ELEPHANT:** *(long pause, then a heavy sigh)* …Fine. What do you want?

**NICK:** *(small smile)* One Jumbo Pop, please.

*[ELEPHANT pulls an enormous popsicle out of the freezer and drops it on the counter. It is as tall as the Little Fox.]*

**ELEPHANT:** Fifteen ninety-nine.

**CUSTOMER A:** *(gasps)* Fifteen ninety-nine?

**CUSTOMER B:** For ice? On a stick?

**NICK:** *(counts out money)* Fifteen ninety-nine. *(hands over the twenty)* Keep the change.

**ELEPHANT:** *(takes it, turns away)* Now go. And don't come back.

*[NICK lifts the Jumbo Pop with both hands. It wobbles.]*

**LITTLE FOX:** *(eyes huge)* It's bigger than me!

**NICK:** *(straining)* Yeah. It's bigger than me too.

*[He turns to Judy.]*

**NICK:** Thanks, officer. That was… decent of you.

**JUDY:** *(cool)* Don't thank me. Just don't make me regret it.

**NICK:** *(winks)* I never regret anything.

*[NICK and LITTLE FOX exit toward stage left. JUDY watches, then follows them.]*

**NARRATOR:** And that should be the end of it. A fox buys a popsicle. A rabbit goes back to work. Everybody is happy. *(beat)* Except nobody is happy. Not yet.

*[Lights change.]*

---

# SCENE 3 — THE SUNNY STEP

**(about 2.5 minutes)**

*[NICK sits on a low block stage left, the Jumbo Pop beside him. LITTLE FOX sits next to him. NICK opens his small bag and takes out a knife, a pan, small molds and sticks.]*

**NARRATOR:** Outside, the sun is hot. Nick does not go home. He sits down on a step. Then he opens a small bag.

**LITTLE FOX:** Dad? Why are you cutting it?

**NICK:** *(working)* Business, buddy. Watch and learn.

*[He cuts the Jumbo Pop into pieces and drops them into the pan. Drip, drip.]*

**LITTLE FOX:** *(alarmed)* But… it's melting!

**NICK:** *(smiles)* That's the plan.

*[JUDY enters upstage, behind them, unseen. She stops.]*

**JUDY:** *(to herself)* What is he doing now?

**NARRATOR:** And then the sun does the rest of the work. Ten minutes later, the Jumbo Pop is not a popsicle anymore. It is a pan of sweet pink water.

*[NICK pours the water into the small molds and pushes in the sticks.]*

**NICK:** *(to himself, pleased)* Perfect.

**LITTLE FOX:** *(poking a mold)* Are those for me?

**NICK:** *(shakes his head)* Those are for customers. *(hands him a small broken piece)* This is for you. *(beat)* Don't eat it too fast.

**LITTLE FOX:** *(licks it, delighted)* Best day ever.

*[Three MICE WORKERS enter from stage left — dusty, tired, tiny tools in hand.]*

**MOUSE 1:** *(wiping his face)* Too hot. I can't work.

**MOUSE 2:** I'd give my whole paycheck for something cold.

**MOUSE 3:** You don't have a paycheck.

**MOUSE 2:** Then I'd give yours.

**MOUSE 3:** *(pushes past him)* Move. I'm buying.

**NICK:** *(bright and friendly, standing up)* Excuse me, gentlemen. Did somebody say "something cold"?

*[He holds up the tray of small popsicles.]*

**MOUSE 1:** *(staring)* Are those… popsicles?

**NICK:** Fresh. Cold. Sweet. Two dollars each.

**MOUSE 2:** Two dollars? That's nothing!

**MOUSE 3:** *(already reaching for money)* I'll take two.

**NICK:** *(handing them out, smooth)* One for you. One for you. And one for the hard-working mouse in the back.

**MOUSE 1:** *(licks)* Oh. Oh, that's good.

**MOUSE 2:** Best two dollars I ever spent.

**MOUSE 3:** *(counts out coins, then hands over three dollars)* Keep the change. For the little one.

**NICK:** *(pleased)* A pleasure doing business.

*[The mice exit, licking their popsicles. One of them is still wearing his hard hat backwards. LITTLE FOX holds the money cup and shakes it. It rattles.]*

**LITTLE FOX:** *(proud)* Listen! Money!

**NICK:** *(winks)* Good boy. You are a very cute son.

**LITTLE FOX:** *(grins)* Thanks! I'm a very good actor!

**NICK:** *(under his breath)* …Shh.

*[JUDY steps forward, arms crossed.]*

**JUDY:** Did he just say "actor"?

**NICK:** *(freezes, then turns with a huge smile)* Officer! Back so soon? Popsicle? On the house.

**JUDY:** No.

---

# SCENE 4 — THE CONFRONTATION

**(about 3.5 minutes)**

*[JUDY crosses to NICK. LITTLE FOX stays between them, looking from one to the other.]*

**NARRATOR:** Judy does not look angry. She looks like a rabbit doing maths. That is much worse.

**JUDY:** How much did you make today?

**NICK:** *(light)* Me? I don't make money. I make friends.

**JUDY:** *(opens her pad)* One Jumbo Pop. Fifteen ninety-nine. *(reads)* You cut it into twelve small popsicles. Two dollars each. That's twenty-four dollars. Plus tips.

**NICK:** *(pause)* …Twenty-one. Two were broken.

**JUDY:** *(writing)* Twenty-one.

**NICK:** *(quickly)* That was private.

**JUDY:** Nothing is private on a public street, Mr. Wilde.

**NICK:** *(sighs, spreads his hands)* Okay. So I made twenty-one dollars. So what? It's a hot day. I sold cold things to hot animals. Everybody wins. Show me the crime.

**JUDY:** *(calm)* The crime is taxes.

**NICK:** *(blank)* …Taxes.

**JUDY:** Taxes. You earned money. You have to tell the city, and pay part of it back.

**NICK:** *(slowly)* Tell the city… that I made money.

**JUDY:** Yes.

**NICK:** *(thinks)* And then give them some of it.

**JUDY:** Yes.

**NICK:** *(long pause)* Why would I do that?

**JUDY:** Because it's the law. Roads. Street lights. The police. Somebody pays for those. Today, that's you.

**NICK:** *(mock surprise)* Oh no. The police. *(looks at her)* Officer, I'd hate to see the police go hungry.

**JUDY:** *(not amused)* You need to fill out a tax form.

**NICK:** *(easy)* I don't have a tax form.

**JUDY:** You can get one at City Hall. It's free. It takes ten minutes.

**NICK:** *(immediately)* I'm not going to City Hall.

**JUDY:** *(writes)* Then I write you a ticket.

**NICK:** *(laughs)* A ticket? For a popsicle?

**JUDY:** For tax evasion. Selling goods. Earning money. Paying nothing. That's not a small thing. I can take you in right now.

**NICK:** *(his smile fades)* You're not serious.

**JUDY:** *(holds up her pad)* I have the price of the Jumbo Pop. I have the empty tray. I have your name. *(beat)* Do you want to try me?

*[Long pause. The Little Fox pulls at Nick's sleeve.]*

**LITTLE FOX:** *(quiet)* Dad? Are you in trouble?

**NICK:** *(without looking at him)* Buddy. Go home. I'll catch up.

**LITTLE FOX:** *(nods, holds up the money cup)* Bye, Dad!

**NICK:** *(calls after him)* Don't call me— *(gives up)* …dad.

*[LITTLE FOX exits, waving. Beat.]*

**JUDY:** *(deadpan)* Cute kid.

**NICK:** *(small smile)* He's not my kid.

**JUDY:** I know.

**NICK:** *(surprised)* …You know?

**JUDY:** He called himself an actor. In front of a police officer. He's new at this.

**NICK:** *(sighs)* He's eight. *(beat)* Okay. Okay, fine. What do you want?

**JUDY:** I want you to fill out the form.

**NICK:** And if I do, you leave me alone?

**JUDY:** Then you pay your tax, and I don't arrest you.

**NICK:** *(carefully)* How much is the tax?

**JUDY:** *(checks her pad, then)* On twenty-one dollars? A few dollars. Maybe less.

**NICK:** *(relieved)* A few dollars. *(pulls out his wallet)* Fine. Here. Twenty dollars. Take it all.

**JUDY:** *(does not take it)* No.

**NICK:** *(stares)* No?

**JUDY:** You can't pay your tax with cash on the street. You have to fill out the form first.

**NICK:** *(slowly)* So you want me to do paperwork… for a popsicle.

**JUDY:** *(nods)* Paperwork. For a popsicle.

**NICK:** *(long pause)* This city is broken.

*[JUDY writes on her pad, tears off the page and hands it to him.]*

**JUDY:** This is a warning. Not a ticket. A warning.

**NICK:** *(reads)* "Please pay your taxes."

**JUDY:** You have one week.

**NICK:** *(reads again)* You drew a smiley face.

**JUDY:** It's a friendly warning.

**NICK:** *(folds it, puts it in his pocket)* And what if I don't?

**JUDY:** *(smiles)* Then I come back. And I bring handcuffs.

**NICK:** *(small laugh)* You're something else, rabbit.

**JUDY:** *(correcting)* Officer.

**NICK:** *(tilts his head)* …Officer.

*[He starts to leave.]*

**JUDY:** One more thing.

**NICK:** *(stops)* Of course there's one more thing.

**JUDY:** I'm keeping this notepad. *(holds it up)* Twenty-one dollars. Twelve popsicles. Your name.

**NICK:** *(uneasy)* …And?

**JUDY:** *(pleasant)* And one day, I might need a small favour.

**NICK:** *(slowly)* What kind of favour?

**JUDY:** *(smiles)* I'll tell you when I need it.

**NICK:** *(a long look, then a shrug)* You know what? Fine. You win this one, rabbit. *(beat)* But I'm not paying for the handcuffs.

*[He exits stage left, hands in pockets. JUDY watches him go, then writes in her pad.]*

**JUDY:** *(to herself)* "Suspect cooperative. Sort of."

*[LIGHTS come up on the full stage. All actors enter and stand in a line.]*

**NARRATOR:** And that is how a fox lost a whole afternoon's profit, a rabbit gained one favour, and a very small actor went home with a free popsicle.

**NARRATOR:** Nick thought he was the smartest animal on the street. Then he met a rabbit with a notepad.

**NARRATOR:** In Zootopia, anybody can be anything. Even a fox. Even a rabbit. Even a fox who pays his taxes.

*[Pause.]*

**NARRATOR:** …Well. Someday.

*[ALL bow. Lights down.]*

---

## NOTES FOR THE DIRECTOR

1. **Pace.** Scenes 1 and 3 are fast and light. Scene 4 is the heart — slow down there, let the pauses work. The "twenty-one" / "twenty" beat and the "paperwork for a popsicle" line are the two biggest laughs; give them space.
2. **Nick's voice.** Keep it low and slow. He should never look like he is trying. When Judy finally wins, he does not get angry — he gets *smaller*.
3. **Judy's voice.** Short sentences. She does not shout. Her power comes from being calm and clear, not loud.
4. **The Elephant.** He is not a villain, just a man with bad habits. Play him big and blunt, not evil. His last line ("don't come back") should get a laugh.
5. **Little Fox.** No acting "cute" on purpose — just do what he says: hop, pull, lick, hold out the cup. His one reveal ("I'm a very good actor") should be said proudly, like a child showing off.
6. **Blocking.** Keep Judy upstage-right of Nick throughout Scene 4, so the audience sees both faces. Little Fox stays between them until he exits — he is the thing Nick is pretending to be good for.
7. **Costume shortcut.** Ears for Judy, a tail and sunglasses for Nick, an apron for the Elephant, a cap for the Little Fox. Nothing more is needed.
